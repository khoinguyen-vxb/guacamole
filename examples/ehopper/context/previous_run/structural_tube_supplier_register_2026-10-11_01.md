## 5. Application to the authorized trade — engineering interpretation

No spine count, leg count, material or tube section is selected here. Compare one-, two-, three- and four-spine arrangements and both tripod and quadpod legs under the current requirements.

- Treat pultruded and roll-wrapped tubing as different materials, not interchangeable carbon tubes. A pultruded candidate needs a credible axial load path and explicit treatment of lateral landing loads, joint eccentricity, torsion and crushing. Do not assume a splayed leg remains axially loaded during first-foot contact.
- Compare whole-stock cutting plans with kerf, end trimming and any genuinely reusable offcuts. Charge each stock length once even when it supplies both spines and legs. Installed lengths and purchased lengths are different quantities.
- Count joint sleeves, inserts, adhesives, fasteners, braces, feet, equipment retention and gimbal attachments in both mass and procurement as applicable. PETG filament cost is exempt, but PETG mass and structural behaviour are not.
- Compare equipment access and retention, bending in both directions, torsion, buckling, joint loads and asymmetric landing cases. Rail separation alone does not establish assembly stiffness without shear transfer through joints and bracing.
- For tripod versus quadpod, compute the support polygon, directional tipping margins, ground-contact assumptions, foot loads and tube lengths from actual candidate geometry. Do not infer stability from foot-span alone.
- Retain bottom-mounted propulsion and the common two-axis gimbal. Assess both rotor envelopes against the legs, attachment plate, spines and ground throughout proposed travel, including landing attitude and unresolved deflection allowances.
- Keep supplier measurements, engineering assumptions and later calculated results separate. Unknown density or modulus must not silently become a validated material property.
